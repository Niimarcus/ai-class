Before class: 3 quick things

Next week we make your portfolio beautiful — animated, polished, the version you actually send. Bring these three things so class is all building, no scrambling.

About 10 minutes
1

Your photo

This is the one that makes the biggest difference — your face on the page.

  • Find or take one clear photo — waist up, facing the camera, good light. Waist-up is what makes the finished page look like the class example: your head lands up in the giant letters. Shoulders-only works too — it just sits lower on the page.
  • Want the floating look from the example? Remove the background free at remove.bg — it hands back a .png; rename it to photo.jpg anyway.
  • Save it as a file named exactly photo.jpg — all lowercase.
  • On your phone: open the photo → Share → Save to Files → rename it to photo.jpg.
  • On a computer: right-click the image → Rename → type photo.jpg.
Any normal photo works — you do not need to cut out the background; the page blends it in for you. No photo at all? Your initials look great too, so this is optional.
⚠️ The name matters: photo.jpg — not Photo.JPG, not photo.jpeg.
2

Three references

People who would vouch for you — a manager, a colleague, a teacher.

  • For each one, jot down: name · their role and where you worked together (company or school) · phone · email.
  • Can't reach three? One or two is completely fine.
1.  name · role & where · phone · email
2.  name · role & where · phone · email
3.  name · role & where · phone · email
Your portfolio is a public page. Only list someone's phone/email if they're okay being contacted — otherwise just write "contact on request" and skip their details.
3

Four quick answers

The practical questions an employer asks. A few words each is plenty.

  • When can you start? — e.g. immediately, or two weeks' notice.
  • Salary? — a range, or just "open to discussion." (You can keep this vague.)
  • Where can you work? — remote, hybrid in your city, or open to relocating.
  • Languages you speak — and how well (fluent / conversational / native).

That's everything. Bring these, and in class one prompt turns them into your finished portfolio.
